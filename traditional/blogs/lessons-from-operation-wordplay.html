Lessons from Operation Wordplay

Pacing a ten-puzzle narrative without burning out solvers.

Operation Wordplay was my longest narrative crossword pack: ten puzzles chasing Word Villain through linguistic chaos. The design problem wasn’t only “write a fun story”—it was hook players early, keep narrative visible between grids, and ramp difficulty without turning the pack into a grind.

We anchored the story on puzzles 1, 5, and 10 before filling the middle. Puzzle one introduces the villain; puzzles two through four show escalating mischief; the fifth delivers a lead; the finale is the chase. That skeleton made editorial decisions easier: every clue had to earn its place on the timeline.

Difficulty followed the same arc. The opening had to be approachable; later puzzles could ask for more. If narrative clues got too cryptic, people quit for the wrong reason—lost in story, not stumped by wordplay.
