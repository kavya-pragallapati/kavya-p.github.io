Minecraft: A Relationship That Is Bound To End In Divorce

February 2023

Minecraft has to be one of the most popular games in gaming history. More than a decade after its first release, it remains one of the most popular titles. It has a loyal fanbase that continues to recruit new players and experiment with mods.

So what is Minecraft? If you're like me, you have only heard of the game without knowing anything about the game. Minecraft is a sandbox game developed by Mojang studios. Created by Markus "Notch" Persson, the game's goal is to survive in a massive open-world randomly generated map. There are no tangible milestones to chase or storyline that intrigues a player. In fact, there are hardly even instructions on how to play the game.

The lack of information provided in the game and how differently every player plays it definitely contributed to my misconceptions about it. Almost two years ago, when talking to a friend, the person who later introduced me to the game, I said, "I thought it was all about combat." I explained to him how I thought the game was purely based on the shoot-and-kill mechanics, and he was beyond surprised at my assumption. He told me it was a relaxing game, and I was not convinced.

Now that I have put a decent amount of hours into the game, I am more qualified to write about it.

One of the things I love about Minecraft is how its gameplay is customizable to each player. There's no set path for me to follow, no milestones to finish, and no mechanics with which I am forced to interact. The game can be played however I like. Which to me means chopping down trees, building something basic and annoying my friends who are on the server.

It's mostly the annoying friends part that I enjoy. Minecraft, at its core, is a game designed to be enjoyed in a community space. Sure, it's fun in a single-player, but it's better when played in a group. Playing in a group also means exploring the various aspects of the game without actually having to engage with mechanics that I do not enjoy. For example, combat is one Minecraft mechanic that I absolutely despise. There's no reason for it other than my unwillingness to learn. I am not someone who enjoys combat in any game. So when I play with a group, I hide behind whoever is the best fighter while they handle the mobs in the game.

But two years after my first conversation about Minecraft, I realize the game is about more than just fighting. When you start a new save on Minecraft, you're dropped into a random world with no resources or tools. To survive, you must forage for everything from food to building materials. It's overwhelming the first time, and it's overwhelming the second time. It would likely take many hours of gameplay before I am at ease with the game's mechanics. I survived because I had pro gamers watching my back and telling me what to do, but something tells me I would have given up very soon if I was playing on my own. The lack of instructions in the game can be frustrating.

The thing I enjoyed the most in Minecraft is the building aspect of it. I spent far too much time in the game, not knowing that I could randomly build whatever I wanted, whenever, wherever I wanted. The experience was life-changing, to be honest, because I genuinely looked forward to the game for the first time. I was thinking about what I would do to the house I was building and planning the resources I would need to do the same. The revelation literally came as a wrecking ball, completely overhauling my perception of the game.

So why, if I love the game, is it a relationship bound to end in divorce? Minecraft is a good game, but it's not for me. There is enough in the game that I love for me to play it for a short duration. But if I were to play it any longer, I start to grow tired and find faults in everything the game offers.

I love the building aspect, but I hate the grind for resources. Yes, there is always the option of creative mode. Still, it feels like cheating somehow and takes away from another aspect I love about the game, resource collection. I hate that resource collection always has to be done manually or requires complicated builds that take far too much effort and investment of time and resources. In short, it's a relationship bound to end in divorce.

Am I perhaps being too harsh on the game? Probably. When I first started playing the game, primarily out of respect for my friend, I was not in the right headspace for serious gaming, let alone learning a game that requires the amount of time and investment required by Minecraft. Not only was I not committed enough to the game, but I was also playing with people who had been playing the game for far too long. For the most part, I was busy trying to keep pace rather than explore the game alone.

To give the game a second chance, I booted it up while writing this review to try it by myself now that I know the basic controls and won't immediately die. But I spawned in a desert with vast amounts of sand to see and not a single tree to punch. Unsurprisingly, I died to creepers thrice in the twenty minutes I played. Let's say the inevitable happened — the divorce.
