Overcooked 2: The World's Most Chaotic Hang Out Session

February 2023

A cooking simulation game developed by Team 17 alongside Ghost Town Games, Overcooked 2 is a co-op game for teams of 4. The gameplay is simple in this top-down simulator, you are thrown into the fast paced service industry as soon as you take control of a character on screen. We gather, prep and cook ingredients mentioned in the recipe, those are then assembled in a plate and served to waiting customers. But, it’s not just about being a chef, you also gather used dishes, clean them and reuse the cleaned dishes for the next round of order. Basically, you run a restaurant.

Just like a real restaurant, Overcooked 2 is not a game you can play alone, unless you’re a pro-gamer or if you have nerves of steel. Since I have neither, the first time I played was with a team. In the co-op mode we, a single team of 4, tried to make our way through the levels.

It tested our coordination skills, our patience and our ability to keep our voices down in the middle of the night. Overcooked 2 is undoubtedly a very well designed game that puts both your mechanical skills and your strategy skills to test. You don’t need to be proficient at the controls if you’re really good at time management. It’s a game that takes ten minutes to learn and hours to master.

The game’s biggest asset is probably its simplicity. The core of the game is simple, but it never feels monotonous or easy. The complexity of the recipes and the multitude of tasks ramp up the stakes in the cooking simulator. The game’s difficulty increases pretty early on, before the end of the first world we were screaming for dishes to be washed, ingredients to be chopped up and sauces to be cooked, and I can tell you from experience none got done on time because we were not just learning to keep up with the game, we were learning to communicate in a way we never had to before.

While the game makes it harder for people to navigate around the level, it also provides abilities that make the game easier. For example, in levels where the kitchen is spread across multiple platforms, all of which will periodically move, cutting off access to certain parts of the kitchen and certain ingredients if you’re not prepared. It’s of course slightly easier when there are a bunch of people, because one person can lock themselves into a part of the kitchen ensuring sustained access to the ingredients placed in the area.

However, having access to ingredients means nothing if we can't pass it onto our team mates, this is where the ability to throw uncooked ingredients comes into play. Being able to toss stuff from one person to another makes the game that much more playable. The ability to throw things around the level map greatly improves maneuverability and creates the opportunity for some very fun moments. It’s always a satisfying moment when I lob a chicken leg straight into the fryer, or toss an ingredient right at my teammate. It is also extremely helpful when I need to thwart their rhythm during a vs match.
