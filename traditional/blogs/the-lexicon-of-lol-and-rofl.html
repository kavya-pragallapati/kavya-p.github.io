The Lexicon of LOL and ROFL

August 2020

A long, long time ago, in a distant past, everyone was fascinated by the invention that changed the world as it was known. That invention was the internet. In the last decade of the twentieth century, which seems like a millennia ago but was only thirty years in the past, my parents learning to use the computers couldn’t have imagined the impact the internet would have on their children. Much less the language they speak and write. It’s no surprise the impact of the internet seeped into our lexicon too.

While the internet has been a significant part of people’s lives for the better part of the twenty-first century, it only recently became an indispensable part of our existence. Since the world has entered a lockdown, people’s lives have shifted online. Everything from work to shopping to hanging out with friends turned virtual.

Five months ago, the little victories I used to share with my friends turned solitary. Shared only through pictures and excited messages on WhatsApp. Four months ago, we started to whine about missing the same classes we despised. Three months ago, the worry shifted towards the fast approaching exams. Two months ago, worry transformed into anger towards the indecisiveness of college administration. A month ago, our paths separated as we all took different routes forward. Now, all news is shared over frantic texts lest it gets lost in the void preceding the weekly call, which only happens monthly.

This new dependence on the internet made me see a reality I had never noticed. We as a generation had developed, long before the pandemic hit, a nuanced language capable of transmitting emotions over text. We condensed emotions expressed through voice, body language, and facial expressions into the alphabet and a couple of punctuation marks.

The universality of this language provides proof for the Psychic Unity of Mankind.

It’s an Anthropological concept put forward by Adolf Bastian, which to me, always seemed a little absurd. The psychic unity of humankind claims that all humans share the same fundamental psychological, mental, or cognitive make-up or potential.

Before the lockdown, there were few examples for it that seemed convincing. Now, however, my friend who has known me for longer than anyone can tell that I disagree with the number of periods that follow my text. Ellipsis has now replaced proper punctuation marks on the internet. None means I am fine, because in the era for instant messaging punctuation takes too much effort. An ellipsis, three or more periods, indicate the silence in which my brain processes the next sentence. Two periods, however, is cause for immediate panic. For years I took this for accepted because she has known me for the better half of my life. Until someone whom I have known for less than three years picked up on the same.

Question marks no longer indicate an actual question but the intonation. The little nuance of the tentativeness of delivering a line. There is no reason for this except, it just seems right?

IMHO, at the turn of the century, this sentence would have made no sense. Acronyms weren’t randomly born. Instead, they were agreed upon, by a committee, before large-scale acceptance. If I hadn’t been from the generation that grew up on this new lexicon, seeing myself use ROFL in a sentence, I would SMH and said ‘askfghlf’ at the absurdity of it.

Everyone tends to see the side of the internet that can’t differentiate between your and you’re while failing to recognize the intricate nuances of the language. It amazes me how with neither written rules nor formal training, people from around the world communicate in a language continuously evolving.

It is without any vocal agreement accepted that the words said, like, and all like have different meanings while communicating the same meaning. While said implies a direct quote from someone, the use of ‘like’ implies that I am paraphrasing. Yet, when I say ‘she was all like’ it means I have added a spoonful of exaggeration, take it with a grain of salt.

When I started writing this, it was a story about human adaptability. Now that I have finished, this is a letter of gratitude to all who helped create an entirely written language capable of capturing the entire spectrum of human emotion. This makes communication so much easier. I can only imagine the pain it would be to not be able to understand if my friend was being sarcastic or simply angry with me.
